Financial Diagnosis 360

A report that reads each store's P&L against similar stores, turns cost gaps into R$ and flags the stores at risk.

Every number on this page is fictitious: an invented network of 60 stores, generated by a script with the same calculation rules as the original reports.

Context

Monthly P&Ls from franchised and company-owned stores arrived in spreadsheets, one per year, with different layouts, flipped cost signs and subtotals without detail. Analysis relied on averaging tabs and manual filters.

The goal was a per-store financial diagnosis in the same spirit as the commercial report: compare with peers, explain the margin and prioritize where to act, while keeping the look the team already used.

What the report answers

  • What is each store's margin and how does it compare with its peers?
  • In which cost groups does the store spend more than its peers, and what is that worth per month?
  • Did the margin improve year over year, looking at the same stores in the same months?
  • Which stores are at risk, and why?
  • Are the submitted P&Ls complete and reliable?

How it was built

It is also a PBIP generated by code. A Power Query function finds the sheet and the data block by its header, so the same reader works for yearly spreadsheets with different layouts. The P&L becomes a long table (store × month × account), and "not detailed" residuals reconcile each subtotal with EBITDA.

What closing the gap is worth

For each controllable group where the store spends more than its peers, the report multiplies the weight gap by the store's revenue. Royalties and deductions are left out because store management does not control them.

What the data showed

  • One year had costs booked with a positive sign; the load converts it to the other years' convention.
  • Subtotals without account detail became "not detailed" lines, so the sum reconciles with EBITDA.
  • The same store and month appeared more than once; a measure counts these submissions for a case-by-case decision.
  • A store that moved had two codes; a mapping table joins its history.
  • Company-owned stores use a different chart of accounts from franchises, which calls for care when comparing the two.

Business rules

Average per store-monthTotal divided by the number of monthly P&Ls with revenue, matching what the team already did in spreadsheets.
Latest registryArea manager and franchisee come only from the latest year's registry and apply to every year, so history does not scatter across former owners.
Vertical analysis baseGross revenue by default, with a selector for sales revenue or net revenue.
Month over monthOnly stores that sent a P&L in both months, including across the year change.
